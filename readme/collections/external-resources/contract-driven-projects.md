# Contract Driven Projects

### Also a reason why they cant adapt Agile thinking

[Anan](https://substack.com/@anan12)Sep 02, 2026

\[Customer collaboration over contract negotiation. Customers discover what they need by using something. Negotiation does not remove that uncertainty.]

[https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7500572081286402050?updateEntityUrn=urn%3Ali%3Afs\_updateV2%3A%28urn%3Ali%3Aactivity%3A7500572081286402050%2CFEED\_DETAIL%2CEMPTY%2CDEFAULT%2Cfalse%29](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7500572081286402050?updateEntityUrn=urn%3Ali%3Afs_updateV2%3A%28urn%3Ali%3Aactivity%3A7500572081286402050%2CFEED_DETAIL%2CEMPTY%2CDEFAULT%2Cfalse%29)

For a long time, this debate has popped up. I had to put it in writing to save my energy. Only now I realize I haven't noted down contract-centric as principle strand.

This is because traditional project initiation and management are viewed through a contract-centric lens, which is foundational in risk and cost management. All of these assume that we know the future, and that's the main failure mode. These are two different schools of philosophy I was referring to. Later, since 2022, I have become accustomed to new frameworks and methodologies to see through or interpret the world around me. My language and grammar changed, but it did not have much effect on the teams. It shifted from one mismatch to another.

If we describe the philosophical schools as old school and modern, the conversation tends to focus on political correctness rather than underlying substance. If you use new methods and frameworks, you'll get objections based on the face value of the names/labels/words they hear. This immediate response always reflects what a word means to them in their personal experience, not a definition, etymology, or a description in a specific context (my earlier post on suspicion). People do not have the time and mental space to spend; they are busy with existing operations. Yet, the conundrum is that we need to slow down and see if we are going on the right path before speeding in the direction we are headed. When you get to the highway, you can achieve better speeds than on a gravel road. Then you need to look at the map. That's only one part, where we can take advantage of what's already been built by others. Then there are places where we have to lay down the paths ourselves; that starts with mapping terrain, which needs communication.

Usually, the incentive structure is one of the important causes, or what we need to change to bring about change. This is very well ingrained in the structure, so it is hard to change from the engine room (read "architect elevator").

Often, teams and teams of teams do not have a good conceptual model to communicate; worse, individuals don't have a mental model to share. Facts are just random, isolated pieces hanging, and an individual themselves can't create coherent sense out of them, so they face a high communication tax due to adding noise by throwing more and more unrelated facts into the context.

What has become ingrained is what worked in the past, and we assume that it will work in the future. In the worst case, people do not question that premise, re-evaluate it, but take that as gospel. All systems are regulated by natural selection. So if parts do not adapt, it will happen at the expense of parts and bring down the whole. That's why empires come and go.

All things start small and would have distributed decision-making, or easily made consensus, or people are in alignment. But when we grow, we face issues, especially communication (read about Dunbar's number).

Usually, the engineering method for scaling is by modularization. Partly because of this, boundaries solidified even after the purpose changed. For example, you can still find project coordinators with checklists without any context, like 80s rules-based execution. The same applies to 2000s DBAs and IT Admins, and similarly in Finance and HR. They do have authority and political capital, which work against modernization. They do not do it with any villainous notion; they are just operating as they are trained to.

This is structured. They have a predetermined way of knowing what knowledge/skills are needed, how to hire, and how to train. This usually involves collecting certificates. Its measurements don't involve the application of knowledge and skill or further ethos and pathos. Traditional hires and rewards are about logos, which is now being replaced by LLMs. In the worst case, it is just memorizing and reciting.

So the whole structure is built on this thinking: We study to score marks. That gives qualification, and based on that, we get a job. In that job, we are told what to do. They send us for training and courses, then award certificates for that. With that certificate, we find another better-paying job, maybe in another country. The country would have controls like resident passes, which is again the same filter as a company. In this scenario, we didn't talk about value creation or value amplification. In another way, net giver or taker. This way is simple because attributing actual value creation to an individual is impossible. As for management, we need to have something in that place, often an illusion of control.

For example, take a company whose financial prudence is built around central ideas. So they have a procurement process. Anything converted to a resource is procured via this process. This treats laptops and engineers the same, in terms of financial categories. If we look through Simon Wardley's mapping, anything that falls under any of the four stages would fall under the same procurement category if the budget falls into the same financial category. So if we hire an engineer, there would be the same exam paper for all, and the same salary range for all, regardless of how well or how poorly an employee performs. This is because it doesn't have mechanisms. Whoever exercises the process would feel it's not their job to change the process. This trickles down; those employees would mark the work done according to what they are asked to do. So nobody checks if the system is working or precisely if the system is adapting, because they are neither authorized nor incentivized. People who have authority won't have any idea what's really happening at the bottom.

The established contract is no longer relevant, and nobody collaborates until the ship sinks or it's too late to react (read about the Titanic and Nokia's fall).
