# An abstraction pays out only in a band

### On Kent Beck being told he is too vague, and why I took almost nothing from Extreme Programming in 2012.

[Anan](https://substack.com/@anan12)Aug 01, 2026

When I step back & try to think (in public) about what's going on at an abstract level I get a lot of push back. "Too vague. Tell me what to do!"\
\
I understand the frustration. And I can't do it. Which is also frustrating.

[https://www.linkedin.com/embed/feed/update/urn:li:share:7482820295322533890](https://www.linkedin.com/embed/feed/update/urn:li:share:7482820295322533890)

Kent Beck gets "Too vague. Tell me what to do!" when he thinks in public. He cannot do it. The people asking are right that they have a part to finish, and detail is what finishes it. I agree with Kent anyway, and this is why.

I read _Extreme Programming_ in 2011 or 2012 and took almost nothing from it. It was not vague. It had values, practices and principles, unlike the Agile Manifesto. My team worked by default. Nothing to attach to. Years later, in a cumbersome organisation where nothing moved, I understood it.

\[!quote] "I'd already largely established my thoughts and habits and my approach to design before Clean Code, the book, was released. And while I didn't really disagree with the ideas, they didn't really seem to add much to what I was already doing."\
Dave Farley, "Clean Code Is Right... It's Just Not ENOUGH" (2026).

[https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/C3UrkqXTftY?rel=0\&autoplay=0\&showinfo=0\&enablejsapi=0](https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/C3UrkqXTftY?rel=0\&autoplay=0\&showinfo=0\&enablejsapi=0)

His point is timing, not quality. He calls Clean Code the most durable guidance of its kind in software.

An abstraction pays out only in a band. Too early and you have not met the problem it solves. Too late and you have solved it another way. Farley was too late for Clean Code. I was too early for Kent's.



The same tree from a leaf and from above; job scope as it is written and as the work sits.

On the tree, stand at B. A reads as related: the path runs inward to the fork they share. C reads as out of scope, its path running outward, though you know C exists. Correcting that needs all of A to H. The layers above still do not follow. Most people do not understand the parts. Job scope is written as mutually exclusive, no overlaps, while the work overlaps, and the mismatch confirms that reading. From the centre, every relation between A and H is there at once. Centre to periphery is easy, the path is known. I have noticed almost nothing in the working day rewards the climb back. The measure is the detail, and once the detail is done your part is done. Understanding was never the thing being measured.

\[!quote] “the difference between the worst taxi cab driver and the best taxi cab driver to get you crosstown Manhattan might be two to one. The best one will get you there in fifteen minutes, the worst one will get you there in a half an hour. Or the best cook and the worst cook, maybe it’s three to one. Pick something like that. In the field that I’m in the difference between the best person and the worst person is about a hundred to one or more. The difference between a good software person and a great software person is fifty to one, twenty-five to fifty to one, huge dynamic range.”

Steve Jobs, Computerworld Smithsonian Oral History interview by Daniel Morrow, 20 April 1995. [https://americanhistory.si.edu/comphist/sj1.html](https://americanhistory.si.edu/comphist/sj1.html)

AI is said to lift the floor, which would compress the range, but that measures the wrong thing. _Measure what the human does, before AI and after._ Before, the human wrote the code; after, the human makes the judgement, so the floor was raised under a task that is no longer the task. And how does anyone judge without the conceptual grasp, which is what this piece has been about, and which most of us never had the opportunity to build? _The range widens rather than narrows_. My own guess, and it is a guess, is that the _best to worst ratio now runs nearer a **thousand/million to one**_ than the hundred to one Jobs gave, and that it varies a great deal with the work. That figure is mine, not his.

The range I saw was mostly in how people learn. I read **Struts 2 in Action** and **Spring in Action** then. The Manning **In Action** books I read open at 30,000 to 50,000 feet, then evolve one topic to enterprise scale. Cookbooks are a different instrument and say so: the **Cookbook** gives “just enough to solve the immediate problem”. Around me were developers with five or six years, consultants and architects with ten or fifteen and more. Struts 2 and Spring Core use was still anti-pattern: validation in the action class, not interceptors. None of their own written. Stack Overflow and tutorial sites answer the question in front of you, never the layer above.

Most of us had read nothing that proved immensely valuable. We had school textbooks under compulsion. We had all read the same things, so nobody around me held information I did not, and I never learned what different information was worth. An abstraction cannot be judged on sight. I could not judge Kent’s either.

**Outliers** reads differently now. Of the 75 richest people in history, Gladwell counts “almost 20 percent” as Americans born within nine years of each other in the “mid-nineteenth century”: Carnegie 1835, Morgan 1837, Rockefeller 1839. He grants them vision and talent, then says they “were given an extraordinary opportunity”. Opportunity gates access, practice converts it, talent counts for least.

I have been called evasive for the same thing. Sometimes in those words. I could not have explained his book to anyone in 2012. I had read it. Which is also frustrating.

"If you can't explain it to a six-year-old, you don't understand it yourself." Some reference this quote, which is famously attributed to Albert Einstein and Richard Feynman.

Another: "Show me how to do it."

The first one mostly comes from a place of authority. The second one mostly parallels. Both tactics are very common ways of shifting burden or not taking ownership at all.

The consequence of the first one is conversation closure, and the second one creates a lot of team spectators, not team players.
