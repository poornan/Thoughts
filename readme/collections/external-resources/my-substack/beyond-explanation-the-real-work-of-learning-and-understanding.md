# Beyond Explanation: The Real Work of Learning and Understanding

[Anan](https://substack.com/@anan12)Feb 16, 2025

Why "Teaching" is Facilitation, and True Comprehension Requires Active Engagement.

***

Do people actively learn from books or long-form articles, or do they prefer passive learning? What if they ask others to explain but simultaneously scrutinize, as if they already know the subject. It's a fallacy—a meta-fallacy. You are scrutinizing the person's ability to explain, not validating the subject itself. A colleague told me that when you properly understand a matter, you can explain it to a six-year-old, citing Albert Einstein: "If you can't explain it to a six-year-old, you don't understand it yourself." If I have to treat my colleague like a six-year-old, how much work would it take to bring him to a graduate level? This assumes my understanding of the subject, ignoring the prerequisites he would need to understand the subject fundamentally, and his attitude and willingness to listen. Nobody teaches, but we learn; teaching is just facilitation.
