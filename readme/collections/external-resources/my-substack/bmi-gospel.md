# BMI Gospel

### Treating BMI as gospel puts us at risk. Always consult experts.

[Anan](https://substack.com/@anan12)Sep 01, 2026

In recent conversations, I see a misconception that looking lean and within BMI is enough. But a normal-weight person with high visceral fat (skinny fat) can face greater long-term risk than someone with a high BMI. BMI is an easier, cheaper way to filter (screen), so we do use it. Treating a screening filter as a verdict is the danger. Treating BMI as gospel puts us at risk.

> Act as a researcher in metabolic health writing for a scientifically literate audience.
>
> Task: Compare the health risks of normal-weight central obesity (the "skinny fat" or MONW phenotype) against elevated BMI, using the visceral vs. subcutaneous adipose tissue distinction as the analytical lens — not as synonyms for the two phenotypes.
>
> Constraints:\
> \- Evidence-based; cite the class of evidence (cohort studies, meta-analyses) where relevant.\
> \- No conversational filler, hedging, or generic wellness advice.\
> \- Use precise terminology: visceral adipose tissue (VAT), subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT), normal-weight central obesity (NWCO), metabolically healthy obesity (MHO).
>
> Address, in order:\
> 1\. Diagnostic failure modes of BMI\
> \- False negatives: normal-BMI individuals with high VAT (NWCO) and their documented excess mortality/cardiometabolic risk.\
> \- False positives: high-BMI individuals misclassified due to lean mass, and the contested "metabolically healthy obesity" category.
>
> 2\. Depot pathophysiology (table)\
> Contrast VAT vs. SAT across: anatomical location and drainage (portal circulation), metabolic activity (lipolysis, adipokine/cytokine secretion), and downstream disease mechanisms (hepatic insulin resistance, chronic low-grade inflammation, dyslipidemia).
>
> 3\. Superior risk metrics\
> List validated alternatives to BMI with their thresholds where established: waist-to-height ratio, waist circumference, fasting insulin / HOMA-IR, triglyceride:HDL ratio, and imaging (DEXA, MRI/CT for VAT quantification).
>
> Note cost/accessibility trade-offs.
>
> Output: dense, structured prose plus the one table. No summary section.
