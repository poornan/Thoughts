# Certification Is Not Contribution

### Certification signals development, not value. Management's job is to tell the two apart.

[Anan](https://substack.com/@anan12)Aug 13, 2026

I heard about an incident recently. A company let go of an excellent, talented contributor. After he gained a professional qualification, the employee asked for a higher salary, and management reasoned that if they granted his raise, they would have to do the same for everyone else pursuing the same certification. So they let him leave.

That reasoning conflates two different things. Most organisations already under-compensate genuine contribution; then, rather than pay for the contribution itself, they wait for an external proxy, a qualification or a certificate, to justify a raise. The certificate becomes the trigger, when the real thing being rewarded should have been the value the person was already creating. How a company handles that distinction is a mere reflection of how it treats its people.

It also misreads how value is created. Contribution is rarely the property of a single person, and it is never the property of a certificate; outcomes emerge from team formation and collective effort. You cannot tie value to one name or one credential and pay against it.

So management has two separate jobs, not one. The first is to assess individual value, the ability to take on more responsibility and accountability, and to pay for that level directly. The second is to fund certification as development: it strengthens the company’s standing as much as the individual’s, which is exactly why the company should help people certify and reimburse the cost.

What this company did instead was quietly let the certificate stand in for a pay rule: certification in, raise out. Allowing a good employee to walk out of the door because you fear having to treat everyone identically once they are certified is management’s failure, not the employee’s.
