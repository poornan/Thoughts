# Dubakoor

### metalinguistic, or more broadly, linguistic description.

[Anan](https://substack.com/@anan12)Oct 05, 2026

The word “Dubakoor” (also spelled Dubakkur or Dubakur) is a prominent slang term in Madras Baashai (Chennai Tamil) used to mean a fraudster, cheat, crook, or someone/something untrustworthy.\
Pop Culture Impact, while the word existed in local Chennai street lingo for generations, it was heavily popularized across Tamil Nadu through Tamil cinema during the late 1980s and 1990s—most notably used as a comedic insult by legendary actor _Goundamani_ to describe cheap, worthless, or untrustworthy characters.

## The Dubash Connection (Popular _Folk Etymology_)

A very common, widely cited theory links the word to the historical term Dubash.

* The Logic: During the East India Company era, a Dubash (derived from the Hindi Do Bhasha, meaning "two languages") was a bilingual interpreter or middleman working between the British and the locals.
* Evolution: Because several Dubashes accumulated massive wealth and gained a reputation for being corrupt, manipulative, or exploiting both sides, the word Dubash gradually became synonymous with a crook or fraudster, eventually morphing phonetically into Dubakoor.

> (Note: While many language experts now consider the Dubash connection to be a folk etymology and favor the "doob/drown" origin (The word derives from the Hindi/Urdu doob - to drown - those who doobed others were Dubakoor), both stories highlight how the word cemented its place in local vocabulary to mean a deceptive character).
