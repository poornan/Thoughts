# It is interesting to see the contrast in the use of the word "know" in situations.

### We know things to project ourselves, yet we do not know enough to do the work.

[Anan](https://substack.com/@anan12)Sep 28, 2026

Almost all of us talk about various subjects without really knowing our competency or performing any basic checks on it, even in subjects we are supposed to know in depth and breadth. We have well-established stances and opinions based on them, then we fiercely protect our positions.

Although we have all the means, such as the internet, to know or to learn, we more easily use "I don't know" or "not my job" as an evasion when it comes to getting things done. Does nobody rise to the challenge or its complexity? Also, are we the people who want to be the human in the loop?

Most organizations just follow trending narratives like agile, cloud, and so on, yet fail. Let's see how we fail in this scenario by looking at a Center of Excellence (CoE). Imagine a company that created a CoE for modernization and transformation. Does a CoE mean the best people? Yet these best people, while having been in the industry for a decade or more, cannot differentiate between time and space when making decisions regarding build, deployment, and runtime. Let's say we are modernizing a build from Ant to Maven and CI/CD without ever reading the literature. Let's imagine we modernize to use the cloud while rejecting automation at every step. Imagine a member of the CoE saying that EAR output must be eyeballed, and when pushed to automate the comparison, responding, "I don't know how to compare two EARs." Imagine requiring a CoE meeting scheduled a week prior, with discussions lasting for weeks, just to remove Maven local repackaging and set standard Maven central GAV, while the code POM changes could be done in hours.

Yet we debate everything, never admitting when we do not know. Only when it comes to doing actual work do we say "I don't know," because that is where knowledge is tested—when it is being applied, not when it is adding noise. How many organizations are buried in noise, taking years to do things that should have been done in days or weeks?

With this in context, how can we redesign work instead of eliminating it? We are already contributing negatively; are we allowing to amplify that?

Something has to be eliminated, but it is in people's hands; external processes can do little. Employees can protect themselves by firing their unfit mental patterns or getting fired; the government must not protect unfit organizations in order to save the country.

[https://www.linkedin.com/posts/anan-elampoornan\_employees-can-protect-themselves-by-firing-activity-7355174983385468928-RKcp](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/anan-elampoornan_employees-can-protect-themselves-by-firing-activity-7355174983385468928-RKcp)

We are very ill-prepared to think about all the above imaginary situations. Those are not pure imagination; that is what happens in more than 90% of companies. In each company, only about 5 to 10 percent of people add, multiply, or create original value. They are very much structural. None of the processes check real value contribution, interviews, manpower procurement processes, EPs, and so on; none of them do.

Try to list down if any process check credential pedigree?

This is the critical problem: how would people with proxy credentials design a system against their own interest while the world is narrowed down to financialization, the only value people are attracted to?
