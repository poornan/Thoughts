# It's a theory: Complains

### It's a theory (not practical/no use)

[Anan](https://substack.com/@anan12)Jan 12, 2025



Structure or behavior comes first? (Did birds develop wings first, or parallel to the behavior of flying? More accurately, were wings developed to fly, where "fly" is the practicality and "wing" is the structure?). Any behavior depends on a structure. Can we have a computer without a mathematical framework, for example, set theory? Set theory did not come into existence overnight, by one individual, and put to practical use as we now use it.

When people say "it's a theory," do they mean "it's a theory in development" and not complete, so it won't have practical use? They are evolutionary; can we find practical uses for what has been already encoded in theory? People often miss that we paid to apply those theories.I don't know why someone would have to pay me to look for a full turnkey solution, and no value is added by me. (To understand this, consider the scenario of copying someone's code versus using a library; note that copying creates a false impression of the original coder who made the high-value contribution.)

And what "practice" means differs in almost all people's minds, because of their purpose, vision, intent, and interest. Anyone who wants a ready-made meal will not think cooking is practical. It is about whether they can make money and more money, pay the bills, feed themselves, and make ends meet—fulfilling immediate necessities driven by instant gratification. Today, anything we call or attribute to a civilized act is handling that temptation; that is what makes humans different from animals.

If you are not involved in creating any structure and only benefit from its fruits, you are not contributing to humanity; rather, you are consuming it. Creating structure is not inherently a selfless act; consider what Tesla earned by inventing AC an unfortunate event. In this context, those who help build, improve, or change structure are the ones who bring progress to society, so an incentive system must be designed to build this culture. We need the right structure to elicit the behavior we desire. Changing existing structure to a desired structure is what "transformation" is.

For Turing, building the theoretical Turing machine was a practical application of theory evolved over 60 years. For many of us today, it is not practical. But that is the platform for many innovations happening today. It's a layer.
