# Navigating the Changing Landscape of Work

### Outcome-Based Contributions and Irreplaceable Value

[Anan](https://substack.com/@anan12)Mar 18, 2025

I was thinking about my past contributions and how the changing climate will impact future contributions.

My most valuable (_**outcome-based**_) contributions were not what the company (manager) tasked me with. From the company's perspective, they value only _**task-based**_ work.

Companies usually ask to have tasks done; my value is _identifying the actual(invisible) task_ and doing it. This is similar to Henry Ford's famous quote, _“If I had asked people what they wanted, they would have said faster horses.”_ Most assigned tasks are like faster horses; most tasks I have signed up for and completed have provided motor cars.

I ensure that my actions consistently reflect my goal of attaining an irreplaceable position in my profession. I developed an acute sense of how my day-to-day work, or any task I undertake, creates outcomes and their _wider impact_. I always search for and acquire new ways to analyze and synthesize and find meaning.

_Isn't that what all people do?_ It's supposed to be, but depending on environment, culture, and incentives, the ratio is shaped. But even in the best case, _it's about 20%_. Let's say, at the time of Nokia's fall, what percentage could it have been? It's all about context.



I have a parallel world, an entrepreneurial endeavor, where I was supposed to give tasks but I couldn't. I set objectives, but my subordinates wanted precise instructions, which was not feasible. Software development, in essence, is about _trial and error_, learning about the system we are building _by experimenting_; Kent Beck says _software grows_. It's not like the old days, where we worked with domains like balance sheets where things could be specified. Even today's accountancy domain can't be specified(_complex situations_) at the very early stage; it should be discovered. If I had known the details at that level, I wouldn't need a team; I could do it myself. We built a team entrusted with autonomy, and they need to own the work. Better work yields better incentives and makes the contributor irreplaceable.

Finding those details is exactly what we _delegate_, that's what we pay for, working code is one output. But if you hire a person for an hourly rate, what _incentive_ will that drive—taking longer or taking ownership? Asking for details or permission is a sign of _resisting ownership and responsibility_. I have seen many use — requests for help, in the form of asking for details or permission, as a technique to _evade responsibility_ or, worse, cleverly _shift the burden_ to others. Then (in 2014), the WSO2 CEO told his employees, when I was working there, "_Don't ask permission; tell me what you are working on._"

If you say you are a developer, you should be able to discover; just knowing syntax is not programming.



[from https://nielsbohrmann.com](https://nielsbohrmann.com/prices-law/)

When a traditional(as a management chartestics) company gets older and/or larger, you can observe Price's Law in action than The Pareto principle.

It started long ago, but for the last five years, I have invested my effort, time, and money in understanding transformations, especially digital transformation. For the last ten years, I have worked in technical debt and modernization. I have added many concepts, methods, frameworks, tools, and tricks to my toolbox over the years.

Sensing, surveying, understanding the situation, then finding the ideal future state and devising strategies to achieve it are the highest contributions one can make. That is, understanding the broader context and contributing to its expansion, not working in a localized context created for one's comfort; mere syntax contributors are already being replaced by GenAI.

With all this, and your ability to use GenAI to augment your abilities, we have a high chance of success.
