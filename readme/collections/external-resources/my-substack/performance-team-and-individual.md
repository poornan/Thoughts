# Performance: Team and Individual

### Remuneration vs. Performance

[Anan](https://substack.com/@anan12)Feb 02, 2025



Figure 1 – Bell Curve / Normal Distribution



Figure 2 – Power-Law Distribution / Paretian Distribution

Remuneration usually follows as the first graph, assuming employee contributions follow this standard distribution. In reality(be aware of the context) employee contributions follow Paretian Distribution. To set context, refer Pareto's law, also known as the Pareto principle or the 80/20 rule, is a theory that states that 80% of outcomes are caused by 20% of inputs and Price's Law: states that the square root of the number of people in a group is responsible for about half of the group's output. Also systems theory, the phrase "the whole is greater than the sum of its parts" means that a complex system, when considered as a whole, exhibits characteristics and behaviors that cannot be fully understood by simply analyzing its individual components; essentially, the interactions between parts create something new and emergent that goes beyond the sum of the individual parts. So tracing back individual contributions with accuracy is hard, but as usual we can look for smells (like code smells in software programming).

When evaluating compensation, be wary if it follows a normal distribution curve, or worse, a narrow normal distribution curve. Similarly, when looking at performance metrics, be cautious if it's not paretian, or worse, a deep and short-tailed paretian curve.

Know all the pros and cons of each measurement and metrics and also be aware of what we don't measure. How do you measure a coach's impact on a team? Then think about team members wearing coach hats, even multiple different hats. Could you assess individuals of what level of knowledge they possess in Bloom's taxonomy: a framework for categorizing educational goals. It was developed in 1956 by Benjamin Bloom and a committee of educators. That brings you to continuous learning.

Both employers and employees should understand these. If the salary negotiation is about economic situation, or personal financial crises and not about the value contribution or future value contribution they can make then we are on the wrong path. Also note that if your base line is based on market average and minimum requirement for visa requirements you're not expecting to hire a high value contributor, that is the context we discuss here. Figure 4 explains why this happens: if the decisions are based on Tradition then less likely we are valuing high contribution and the assessment is based in Logical and Scientific Method, you likely won't fall in this trap. Example, if a developer's performance is measured by how many jira cases/ DCR being handled, then the system won't have any properties of good design. Good design is hard to measure directly, it can only be measured by proxies.

Main reason I write this is to understand how we can transform. First part is to understand what the underlying structure and power influence, assumptions and beliefs. Build shared mental models to understand the current state and the desired state.

Update 1.

Recommendations for employees.

1. Do not list duties and responsibilities as in Job Descriptions in your CV. tell what you really did during the tenure with impact, mainly what you have done outside your day to day expected job. Any initiatives you have taken, then this makes a world of difference in value contribution. STAR is a useful method. If you can't do so, you don't really know your value.



Figure 5

2. Goal setting - to make a high value contribution you need to take ownership and responsibility. Companies should provide autonomy and sponsorship. For that, you need to understand and comprehend the industry, business domain, regulatory, legal and ethical matters in general. Also, companies Vision, Mission, values, principles and roadmap. These are not static, you need to be able to contribute.Early goal setting and agree on how these are measured, devising strategy is key to success. The SMART goal is a useful method.



Figure 6

3. When accepting job offers, make sure your skills and experience are valued and proportionally compensated otherwise you might experience a pygmalion effect.

A map can also be a snapshot from a point in time, representing something that no longer exists. This is important to keep in mind as we think through problems and seek to make better decisions.
