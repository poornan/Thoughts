# Professionalism and code of conduct are key judgement criteria rather than techniques and skills

### when people have right attitudes and character they pick up right skills and techniques for the situation; not about what one already knows but what they can know

[Anan](https://substack.com/@anan12)Sep 22, 2026

> I have published as note first, then noticed some text did not fit in, so this article form.

S’pore commuters who blast loud audio, put feet on bus seats can be fined up to S$500 from Sep. 23 \[[https://mothership.sg/2026/09/singapore-commuters-loud-audio-feet-bus-fined/](https://mothership.sg/2026/09/singapore-commuters-loud-audio-feet-bus-fined/)]

This prompted me to recall a cascade of thoughts I have had over the years.Back in the early 2010s, I was very attracted to Uncle Bob’s work. A large population knows the SOLID principles, as reciting them was key for interviews at the time and still is now, but they may not know Uncle Bob or his works; the Clean Coder and Craftsmanship movements, led by the same person, provided mnemonics. To go beyond reciting and to practice, one must know the background, as practicing demands comprehension of broader literature so one would know of Robert C. Martin and his work.

He was vocal about self-regulating the profession. His core argument is that the software industry must regulate itself through strict professional standards and ethics before a major software catastrophe forces governments to step in and do it blindly. I had a Facebook page called Programmers’ Club (now removed by Facebook for reasons unknown to me) where I tried to build a local community with a professional code of conduct based on the BCS standards. Also reproduced his work on \[[https://blog.inception.lk/2017/12/programming-as-profession-part-ii.html](https://blog.inception.lk/2017/12/programming-as-profession-part-ii.html)]

My point here is that most of these concepts of right and wrong are actually shades of gray and are only meaningfully determined by context, which is then judged by common sense, civic sense, professional ethics, duty, and general moral values. The debate always arises where there is no crime, but rather a breach of professional ethics or fiduciary duty. As we move all managerial and administrative tasks to automation and AI, what is left for us now is human decision-making, as accountability and responsibility heavily fall under this.

As Uncle Bob put it, we must self-regulate, or the government will force one upon us, which is an impedance mismatch.Because all or 99% of managerial and administrative tasks are automated, there is less we can do with centralized decision-making, other than centralized strategies with decentralized execution, which prompts people to have ingrained moral and ethical values rather than just certificates with techniques. This changes how we choose leaders compared to how we recruit employees. Modern employees are leaders, but the whole system and most societies still try to recruit leaders like employees, which is why those efforts fail.

For example, when TT was introduced, if all relevant parts of the government had interpreted the change in their context, what was later found to be a contradiction would have been resolved much faster without expending much political capital. And solutions to future events like this is not more Central bureaucratic methods to contain but more decentralised leadership than compliant employees. So now, attitudes and character are more important than skills and techniques, which is often found in entrepreneurship.

It is more important to know values and make judgments than to rely on the traditional, centralized understanding of codifying “malum prohibitum” (wrong because it is prohibited); why it is wrong matters more, just as knowing the background literature does, than a mnemonic like SOLID.

Applying the same in Procurement domain. Traditionally Procurement is more apt for utility/commodity so we must not rely on few suppliers. Then for genesis, and product context, these are complex domain so such use of standard commodity like Procurement bring extremely bad expensive outcomes.

Example you cant use the same process like commodity for recruit software Engineers; two engineers with same qualifications shall contribute extremely different scale (Steve Jobs famously stated that the performance ratio between an average person and the best in most fields is at most 2:1, but in software and creative engineering, the difference between an average developer and the best is _50:1 to 100:1_). There is no crime in using the wrong process, but rather a breach of professional ethics or fiduciary duty.

We do not recognize that because we are more like the boiling frog; according to Kaufman, service excellence is not a static staircase—it is an escalator continuously moving downward, so if you stand still or merely maintain your current service standard, your value decreases in the eyes of your customers because their benchmarks and competitor capabilities are always rising.

More rules and more fines will do little in the future; only attitudes and character in the community value system will succeed in the AI era.
